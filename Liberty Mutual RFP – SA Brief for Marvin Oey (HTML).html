3What Liberty Mutual wants

Not a website redesign: an enterprise content platform. Their gap is not delivery (they already have headless delivery, JSON APIs, templates, search and personalization). It is business autonomy with governance: intuitive authoring, visual preview, approval workflow and governed publishing, plus content that is AI-ready by design so Liberty stays the cited source in AI answers.

  • North Star themes: empower the people closest to the content; build once, reuse everywhere; innovate without compromising trust.
  • Scope: libertymutual.com, agents.libertymutual.com, comparioninsurance.com and the Independent Agent (IA) Portal (two properties). US users only. Spanish content required.
  • Scale (placeholders per the tab): dotcom 85k peak concurrent, 20.5M monthly page views, 641 pages, 12,255 component entries, 15.4k images. IA Portal 200k authenticated users, 4M monthly page views, 100k+ content items. About 10 authors per property (40 editors and 874 saves on a peak dotcom day). Targets: 99.999% availability with zero maintenance downtime, under 400 ms for consumer pages, 0.5–1 s for the IA Portal.
  • Their stack: Next.js, React and Angular front ends; a backend-for-frontend layer; Apigee; Akamai; Coveo and Apache Solr; Entra ID and Ping; Datadog, Splunk, CloudWatch; Heap, Contentsquare, Adobe and Google analytics; Profound (AI visibility).
  • Scoring: functional fit, technical fit, supplier viability, cost (weights not disclosed). The RFP asks vendors to say where the platform is system of record, where it integrates, and where add-ons are needed. That candor is scored.
  • Demo criteria: insurance depth first; use their data and their sources/destinations where possible; investing in the platform over the next 2–3 years; trusted, AI-ready content. They explicitly score separation of current vs roadmap functionality.
  • Contract points: they want to contract only with Optimizely for implementation support (Sec. 6C), so any third party, such as Slalom, must be disclosed. Offer valid 120 days with in-service within 12 months. They ask us to accept their standard AI terms (CP-25).

4Your demo: what the Demo Guide asks for

Agenda (Demo Guide slide 4, 90 min): introductions 5 · company and product overview 5 · case studies 10 · Q&A 5 · live use-case demo 45 · Q&A 5 · roadmap preview 10 · Q&A and wrap-up 5. The RFP (section 7) also lists a technical/architecture review. Slide 9 asks for the roadmap to cover the next 2+ years.

Use case 1 · LibertyMutual.com

“Trusted Answers, Wherever the Journey Begins”

  • Natural-language question returns an approved, cited answer, with a fallback when uncertain
  • Structured fields, taxonomy, metadata and schema that make content findable by search and AI engines
  • Marketer builds and publishes a page from pre-approved components, with preview, approval, versioning and audit, no engineering ticket
  • Newly published content auto-indexes into enterprise search (with product, state, audience metadata)
  • Profound → CMS pattern: content from Profound enters through CMS workflow and governance
  • Same content served to a second experience through a stable, versioned API
  • Quick-answer analytics (page visits, clicks, scroll depth) for marketers

Use case 2 · Independent Agent Portal

“One Relevant Liberty Experience”

  • Two agents with different role / state / agency see different product content and bundles
  • Marketer changes a priority with no code or release and the agent view updates
  • Preview as two agent audiences before publishing
  • Governed templates and components keep every portal page consistent; a component change propagates safely
  • A disclosure updated once appears on both the IA Portal and LM.com
  • Author / approve / publish role separation, audit trail and rollback

For every use case (Demo Guide slide 9): show the user interface step by step, the underlying data controls and configuration, current and future AI capabilities, and in-platform data governance for user choice and suppression. A separate Demo Flow page has my proposed run of show.

Liberty demo audience (about 26): includes a Data Protection Officer, a Platform Architect, a Solutions Architect, principal and senior engineers, a senior test engineer, a UX Director, a Product Owner, marketing and procurement. Expect deep technical questions.
